About the Program

We give kids
the spark.

Change Makers is a two-week summer program built around one belief: kids are not too young to fix things. They just need the tools, the space, and someone who actually believes that too.

Our mission

Give kids the skills, space, and belief to start improving their world — now, not someday.

Most programs teach kids about problems. Change Makers teaches them to act on them. Over seven sessions, the group goes from asking “what bothers us about the world?” all the way to standing up and presenting a real project they built together.

The program isn't soft. It asks kids to make decisions, handle setbacks, work through unclear problems, and speak in front of others. These are the things that build actual confidence — not the participation-trophy kind, but the earned kind.

🎯

Motivation

Kids don't commit to things because adults think they should. They commit when the work connects to something they genuinely care about. We help them find that thing — and fan the flame.

🛠️

Knowledge

Caring isn't enough. Knowing how to research, plan, collaborate, and execute is what turns good intentions into actual outcomes. We teach the craft of making change, step by step.

💡

Self-Belief

The most common reason people don't try is that they don't think it'll work. By the end of the program, students have proof — their own proof — that they can make something happen.

Why here

Why Mecca, specifically.

Every year, millions of people from every corner of the world come here. They see how we live. How we treat each other. How we take care of our city and our community — or don't. That's not a small thing.

The children growing up in Mecca are growing up in one of the most watched, most significant communities on earth. What they build here — how they act here, what they care about here — carries weight that extends far beyond what any of us can immediately see.

We are stewards of this place. And stewardship, like everything else, is a skill that has to be learned. Change Makers is where that learning starts.

The program structure

Seven sessions.
One real project.

Each session builds on the last. By the end, students don't just have a project — they have a new way of looking at the world.

1
What is a Change Maker?
Intro session: stories, examples, and the mindset shift that starts everything. What does it mean to actually do something?
2
Choose the Project
The group comes together to identify one or two real problems they want to tackle — in their neighbourhood, city, or community.
3
Research It
Go deeper than the surface. Understand why the problem exists, who it affects, and what's already been tried.
4
Plan
Turn understanding into a concrete action plan — with real steps, timelines, and resources.
5
Execute
Do the work. This is where the program earns its name — the group carries out their project in the real world.
6
Document
Record what happened, what worked, what didn't, and what they learned. The story matters as much as the result.
7
Present
Stand up and share. Final presentations to an audience — the full story from problem to solution to reflection.

Who this is for

Kids who notice things.
Kids who ask "why."
Kids who are ready.

Change Makers is for children aged 9 to 14 who have opinions about the world around them. They don't need to have all the answers. They need curiosity, a willingness to try new things, and the ability to work with others — even when it's uncomfortable.

We look for three things in applicants: Are they willing to try something new? Are they able to work as part of a team? Are they willing to step outside their comfort zone, even when it's hard?

That's it. We don't require experience. We don't require a specific type of intelligence. We require the courage to show up and actually try.

"We are not the ones who will fix this world. But we can choose to be part of how it gets better — and we can raise children who make that choice."

The goal of this program is not to produce world-changers. It's to produce girls who are willing to be used for something good. Who show up when it counts. Who try, even when it's hard. Who understand that the work is bigger than any one of them — and do it anyway.
